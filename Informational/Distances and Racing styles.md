So before we crunch the stats, let's talk about **Distances** and **Racing Styles**. Distance is quite straight forward, so we'll start there.  
  
Uma races have four distance categories: **Sprint, Mile, Medium and Long** from shortest (1000m) to longest (3200m). I almost consider the difference between the shortest Long track (2500m) and the longest (3200m) enough to suggest a Super Long rating, but that's besides the point (especially because Ruby is _not even remotely capable_ of distance running).  
  
**Sprint** racers need raw power and speed. With such a short distance, going at full sprint for the whole race is pretty reasonable for even Uma with minimal stamina, so the quicker your max sprinting speed is, the better. Equally important is your acceleration - these tend to be a battle of inches, so not only is explosive power out the gate important, another burst of power down the home stretch is just as critical for strong performances. Thus, we want **Acceleration** and a high **Speed** attribute. These are simpler in the grand scheme of racing, but they're _extremely_ demanding on these two stats.  
  
**Long** racers are on the other side of the spectrum, where stamina and willpower are key. It's the old adage of it being a marathon and not a sprint. This is the domain of not only the physically robust Uma, but also for the strategic minds, as managing your pace and remaining coolheaded under pressure are vital. Top speeds are less of an issue here, because it's more about being able to maintain a consistent pace so that you have something left in the tank on the final stretch. This is the most technical of races, and requires a wide berth of skills: **Breathing, Pain Threshold, Racing Tactics** and **Composure,** plus **Stamina**, of course. Let it be known that stamina tends to be the be-all-end-all and is the most minimum requirement for success at this distance.  
  
**Mile** and **Medium** naturally fall in between these two, Mile leaning towards the powerful speedster side, Medium leaning to the intelligent workhorse side, both without being as extreme as Sprint/Long.  
  
Now for the styles. We have **Front Runners, Pace Chasers, Late Runners** and **End Closers**.  
  
**Front Runners** burst right out of the gate, relying on their **Speed** and **Acceleration** to fight to lead the race, quite literally, so as to break out from the pack and _stay_ out of the pack. Once they are at the front they are fully in control of setting the pace, attempting to mess up the tempo of the other three racing styles by forcing them to exert themselves to keep up and ruin their game plans. This is quite a sensory-reliant role, as just running at full tilt the entire time is wasting the cerebral element of the position, so some degree of **Racing Tactics** is a good idea, and I'd say **Instinct** (to a lesser degree than other roles which can really capitalise on windows of opportunity) too, plus a good deal of **Composure** to fend off hungry Pace Chasers looking to pounce.  
  
**Pace Chasers** pick a front runner and shadow them right up until the end of the race, where they try to find an opportunity to burst past them at the last moment. They can get away with less acceleration than other positions since they don't have to fight for the lead initially, and they have the second shortest distance to sprint given their forward position. Instead, they have to be as terrifying as possible - they have to be difficult for Front Runners to throw off, perhaps by being **Sneak**y or by using **Subterfuge** to make their opponents misread them, or by just having the **Stamina** and **Composure** to deal with the fact they're not in control of the race for the majority of it, they need **Intimidation** and **Frenzy** to shock front runners when they do make their move to throw _them_ off and prevent a contest in the final stretch and they need good **Racing Tactics** and/or **Instinct** in order to find said opportunity to begin with.  
  
**Late Runners** are just chillin'. They sit in the middle-back of the pack and do their own thing, not exerting themselves too hard until the final stretch where they take advantage of their superior condition to break out of the pack late and finish extremely strong. They are prone to being "boxed in", that is surrounded in front and on the sides, meaning they can struggle to take advantage of all that stored power late in the race, thus **Dodge** and assumedly **Dancing** are key. However, this is still easier said than done - one still needs to find an opportunity to strike, and when you can have upwards of 10 Umas in front of you, without strong **Racing Tactics** it can be hard to pick the right time and place, plus you need to be able to **Accelerate** enough to make up for lost time being towards the back of the race. I think interestingly this is a great place to make use of **Creativity** - finding sick racing lines to break out in an unpredicatable way sounds very Late Runner-y.  
  
Finally, **End Closers** are more extreme Late Runners. They just chill right at the back until before the final stretch at which point they say "fuck it", go right around the pack and zoom down the final straight on the outside. **Acceleration** is more important than ever even though they get plenty of track to accelerate because they have a lot of catching up to do once they do make their move. This is also an extremely cerebral position, however it is less of an emphasis on the overall state of the race and more about understanding one's self. Funnily enough, this is where you want those **Visual Mathematics** numbers - calculating on the fly how much you can output will help you figure out when you need to start pressing the gas.  
  
IMO, our stats are spread pretty unfortunately for really any kind of distance/style combination. My instinct says we might be looking at a **Pace Chaser** or **Late Runner**, likely for **Mile** distances (1500m-1800m), although part of that is about how much we can "cheat" with skill checks, because every style and every distance is missing an important stat or two.

First immediate concern is one that was pointed out in Chargen - Ruby has some super ordinary racing stats overall, and the biggest problem is that her strengths and weaknesses are in direct opposition to one another. Let's talk about the biggest offenders:  

- A honestly _miserable_ **Acceleration** stat, which is absurdly restrictive. We can't contest the lead at the start of the race to be a Front Runner, we can't accelerate to make up distance deficeits as an End Closer, and we don't have the explosive power for Sprints.
- Next up in the disaster zone, a less-than stellar **Breathing** and even worse **Pain Threshold**, two of what seem to be the most important long distance-related skills. This completely locks us out of Long distances and will punish us for doing Mediums, so we're kinda forced into Mile races. It also screws with our style choices - it makes going Pace Chaser tough as it is the most stamina-demanding position... which _really sucks_ because it's the position which best mitigates our bad Acceleration.
- Very respectable **Dodge** supported by her extremely impressive **Dancing**, which would make for a great **Late Runner...** Except our bad acceleration rears its ugly head yet again! Late Runners simply cannot function without strong acceleration skills.

Our biggest strengths, Dodge and Dancing, are brutally wasted thanks to our awful Acceleration. Very cool.

1. Take +**Stamina**, giving us a better chance at running as a **Pace Chaser** to take advantage of its low Acceleration demand and getting value out of our Frenzy, Composure, and to some degree Sneak and Subterfuge. As a bonus, we should be more firmly in the stamina bracket to run Mile races comfortably, which is an added bonus.

This unlocks our best attributes, our Dancing and Dodge skills, and is a pretty flashy position given the kinda turn-around 'victory from the jaws of defeat' endings we can force. This kinda shifts us further towards running Sprints instead, so it's not a problem we are 'sacrificing' stamina by taking it.
