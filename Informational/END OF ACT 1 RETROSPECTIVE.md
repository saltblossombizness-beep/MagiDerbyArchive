Six months, 144k words and over 800 pages later, and we've arrived at the end of ACT 1 of Umamusume: Magical Derby. It's been a considerable journey, to say the least. It certainly wasn't what I'd envisioned when I was conceptualizing the quest in my head on a random plane flight back from a con all those months ago. Readers of my previous quest, Puppeteer Princess, might remember these roundups I did for every major section of the story, where I talked about what I liked and didn't like and hoped for the future. This will be more of the same, and it's going to get a bit rambly, so buckle in.  
  
**The Beginning**  
The origin of this quest isn't super interesting. I heard about Umamusume when the game dropped in global. I picked up the anime and consumed all three main seasons, then moved onto Cinderella Gray. I played the game on my way and during the anime con, which was so far early that the only uma content there were the Shadowverse cards one of the vendors was selling. Somewhere along the line, the idea of a quest simulating the mobile game's management-style girl-raising loop fell into my head.  
  
So off to work I went.  
  
Mechanics went first. After messing up a lot with Puppeteer Princess, I was determined to set a solid foundation before proceeding further. The AGG-style was chosen due to its emphasis on making gains and raising stats. The scope of them in other quests was kind of daunting, but I'd attempted to compress them in another quest (Skills and Freaks) to not much success due to ambiguity and the Talent system I wielded having less flexibility than I'd hoped. This time, I'd commit to the numbers. I drafted a quick introduction and got busy constructing a stat system, taking some influence from Puppeteer Princess on this and a lot from Fallout's SPECIAL system to match with the six key stats of Umamusume.  
  
Overall, it's been decent. The 4+ skills for each category have proved to fit a wide variety of situations (though some are used less than others). Players came up with seriously creative builds in the second part of the character creation when Traits were introduced, especially with the Skilled and Gifted traits. It's a shame they'll unlikely to be used. The Racing skills I'm pretty satisfied with. I remember having some trouble conceptualizing the last two. Nutrition hasn't been used as much as I'd hoped, so that's something I'll try to solve for ACT 2.  
  
One thing I was adamant on was introducing an extra element to distinguish the quest from the game. At the time, there were exactly zero fanfics on Spacebattles. I thought that people wouldn't read the quest or get bored with it soon if it was just an OC going through Tracen. Hence, I needed something familiar to help readers settle in, so in came the magical girl element. And from that: Plain Jane and the Nightmare. The real hardest part was writing the first chapter. Writing characters are good, except when you have to write a fully generic one that doesn't have a solid lens to the world, a history, or hope for the future. More on that trio later.  
  
Did you know that I wasn't intending on writing canon umas? That's why I requested permission for me to use the character plans that didn't make the cut. Puppeteer Princess and Skills & Freaks had both been original quests, and I had been fully planning on writing OC umas first and foremost. But then came Rudolf, Narita Brian, Grass Wonder, Oguri Cap…Umamusume got super popular, I learned about how these umamusume were written and…bye bye that plan!  
  
I never could have predicted just how interested I'd be in these silly horse girls and their RL counterparts' histories.  
  
**Satono Ruby and writing her**  
Some readers have expressed Ruby's conflict is interesting enough to standalone as its own story. I could ask no better proof that I've done something right with her. A year and a half or so ago, I'd thought Princess Gloria was as good as my heroines were going to get. But I think Ruby has since surpassed her, and I'm not saying that because her quest is more popular. Through the scenes I've written and how she interacts with her peers, she feels like a more well-rounded character in terms of how she views life, expresses emotions, shifts moods, interacts with her hobbies, and the other many pieces that consist her life  
  
The primary method I used to help define her was compartmentalised by Wildbow. Yes, that Wildbow of Worm fame. Yes, yes, I know he's quite infamous on this site. I wasn't a fan of Ward either. No-one writes that many millions of words without knowing something about writing compelling characters, so put down the torches and pitchforks. The framework he employs is called the Snowman, the explanation of which I shamelessly copy and pasted from his blogpost [here](https://palewebserial.wordpress.com/2023/11/04/essay-creating-pale/) (Spoilers for Pale):  
  

> **Background – (Past)** – Where the character comes from, their demographics, family, touchstone moments (like the islands in Inside Out, for those who've watched that) that they keep going back to, the foundations of who they are.  
> **Lens – (Present)** – How they view the world, how they interact with it? What do they prioritize or focus on first, given the choice?  
> **Goals – (Future)** – What do they want, what do they need? Those are two very different things. It also includes the short term and the long term, major obstacles to overcome (or not overcome).

  
This is basically a three-axis foundation for how a character ticks inside.  
  
We can apply Ruby to this framework.  
**Background:** a former daughter of the prestigious Satono family, exiled to the shrine as a shinme-in-training, suffered a harsh life before running away to become an underground umadol in the big city. She's solidly in the impoverished runaway teen demographic. She got attracted to the idol and youth scene in Shibuya and the rest of Tokyo's main wards. She has more experience with women than men due to working closely with an idol group for most of her adolescence.  
  
**Lens:** Because Ruby is an impoverished teen, she doesn't trust adults as easily and doesn't waste food. Because she suffered in early life, she's extremely grateful for her opportunities. Because the city welcomed her at first and she was lucky to find Manager Suzuki, she attained a positive mindset despite the hardship.  
  
**Goals:** Ruby wants to win a G1 and dance on stage while wearing her mother's shoes. To achieve that, she must train and attend Tracen's classes in the short-term while winning races and making friends in the long-term. As for what she needs…that leads into spoiler territory. The want and the need are two sides of a coin. A character's want is rooted in what other guides have called the 'shard of glass' or 'ghost'. This is a pain point that develops an inaccurate worldview (or misbelief) in the character, which drives them to take action through the plot, seeking something they believe will fix this pain point.  
  
The need is the antidote to that misbelief and is generally what the character must gradually learn over the course of the plot, instead of their original goal. So if I told you Ruby's need, you'd know exactly how her journey would end! Have this [guide](http://blog.janicehardy.com/2021/05/making-sense-out-of-character-wants-and.html#:~:text=Understanding%20that%20need%20is%20what,is%20to%20be%20left%20alone.) that uses Shrek instead.  
  
Once these axes are defined, we can apply them to scenes. Characters live in the present and aspire to the future, stemming from what happened in the past. Their decisions are thus affected. Let's use Ruby again and bring up the absolute disaster in the coffee house. Ruby wanted to reconcile with Diamond, despite the warning signs, because her Lens taught her to approach life positively. She felt uncomfortable at the wealth Diamond put on display because her Background is an impoverished one AND she used to belong to that world as a Satono. She hung around hoping for the best because part of her Goal was to make lots of friends in Tracen, but once the topic of the shoes was brought up she realised that her desires were irreconcilable with Diamond's desires. Yummy, yummy conflict.  
  
Cue the fork striking down from Oguri.  
  
The writing of the scene itself was obviously more complicated. Dialogue, prose, emotional behavior and more. But that's the framework I used. Write your character long enough and those three axes ingrain themselves into you subconsciously.  
  
The Snowman is not a all-encompassing checklist of how a character is built. It doesn't show how to write their voice or how they are designed visually. It is, however, a solid foundation to help you work out those additional details. I use it in conjunction with other frameworks (that I'll leave secret for now). The Snowman can also be mapped to existing characters, which is what I did for canon umamusume.  
  
_Agnes Digital (ingame):_  
  
**Background:** Grew up with a father who owned a printer company, was exposed to the umamusume racing side at an early age. Admired umas so much she developed into a super uma otaku and embedded herself in Japanese otaku culture.  
**Lens:** Loves, loves, loves umas. Super interested on watching their adventures and exploits from afar. Uma-otaku-brained, but has set herself hard boundaries as to not intrude on her beloved umas.  
**Goals:** Wants to simp umas from afar. Needs to learn that she can appreciate and respect them by competing with them in races.  
  
(Quest Digitan has progressed past that point and has a different set of axes.)  
  
Not all characters receive the same amount of depth with the Snowman, but I do write an outline for each of them.  
  
Going back to Ruby, another goal I had was to not make her genki all the time (think Pecorine from Princess Connect). I wanted her to feel upset, fed-up, confused, awkward or nostalgic while still retaining her positive outlook. Scenes like her snapping bluntly at Nakayama Festa and the Mood drops when meeting Digitan were intended to reinforce that.

**Other characters**  
Grass Wonder, Oguri Cap, Agnes Digital, Symboli Rudolf etc. There's a layer of interpretation when it comes to writing pre-existing characters. We'll always project our own biases onto them. Left unchecked, the characters will become nothing but mouthpieces for the author's opinions. You may have already read fanfic like this (or some shitty Twitter RP account). This fear, combined with my preference for writing my own universes, is the reason why I never bothered writing Wormfic or much 'standard' fanfic at all. But writing existing characters became unavoidable when I actually got into entering Tracen and my original plan of pure OCs shrivel up and died.  
  
Luckily, the reception to how I've written canon characters in this universe seems to be generally positive, so chalk another one up to the QM. The wide variety of translated material helped a ton. The Satonos were the most controversial, due to me depitcing true ugliness on the otherwise sweet Satono Diamond. I got her mostly fine the first time, I think? The Akiko incident was a blunder on my part and she's still being redone. Crown was okay. I tried my hardest not to make the Satono girls hate sinks. Hatred is rarely a force of absolute evil. It is born from human jealousy, spite, sadness or rage, and we can all justify it as long as we dehumanize the opposition. There's a real murky depth to this prestigious umamusume family. It's a lot of original backstory to be written on my end. The SEGA analogy has given me some material to work with, thankfully.  
  
As an aside, for a gacha girl-collecting game, the umamusume are shockingly well characterized. Digitan's uma-simping fangirl nature seems like a silly meme until you realise it's linked to her confidence issues that she spends her career learning to overcome. Having a consistent artstyle and a wide range of bodily motions in cutscenes also really, really helps.  
  
I enjoyed making my OCs. People liked the FAITHFUL girls, despite only appearing in flashbacks, which was a pleasant surprise. Writing Plain Jane was always a joy.  
  
**World**  
Tracen's a pretty fun setting to explore. There's a natural silliness inherent to the school through its colorful cast and the sheer bloody focus it has on umamusume racing (SERIOUS BUSINESS GUYS). We've got gigantic track rollers, shady acupuncturists, ghosts in the night, totally-not-adult-women and private militaries all wrapped up and bumping shoulders in a single campus the size of several universities. It let me write fun anime hijinks without tending towards dark humor my previous quests did, which was a big refresher.  
  
It's also not so surreal or flat that heartfelt moments feel tonally dissonant. Comparisons to Yakuza are apt. Life is tragic yet fun yet boring and it comes at you one segment after another without pause. See also: Cinderella Gray.  
  
Please don't ask about how time flows in relation to every umas' career. Cygames isn't answering. Neither will I. 20XX exists for a damn good reason.  
  
The Nightmare and magic is all original content from my end. But it does have roots in the supernatural aspects of Umamusume, such as Gold Ship's Eden, Marvellous Sunday's reality-warping abilities and Manhattan Cafe's friend. I'm just taking the Pretty out of its Derby. Look forward to seeing it advance and barrage our beloved school more in ACT 2. The statement I made about the tone being similar to mainline Persona holds.  
  
People die in Persona. Don't forget that.  
  
The magic system was developed from existing ideas I had for other projects and a bit of Puppeteer Princess. Developing it will be fun.  
  
**Plot**  
Puppeteer Princess had decent character moments,, but I got carried away writing them, and as a result, the plot slowed to a crawl. You know the author's messed up when the first major objective is only resolved like 200k words in.  
  
I resolved to improve on this with Magical Derby. Ruby got her first goal (the tryouts) set up in early Encounter 1 with the magical world hinted long before that. Most of the following content was building up to that. The Diamond meeting delayed this, but since it was related to Ruby's backstory I thought it was appropriate. The tail end of Encounter 2 and the Daily Life sections of Encounter 3 might've fallen back on bad habits (words words words). I'd like to use the excuse that webnovels inherently sprawl but…eh, there's a difference between sprawl and flooding.  
  
The Nightmare I also could have foreshadowed better. I sprinkled in hints of mysterious and soul-crushing stuff. Something to string them along could've worked. I could say might not have connected them due to her being so focused on training. It's my job as a QM to make her connect those dots, however. But the quest format doesn't allow for mass back-editing so it is what it is now. Maybe I could have another Plain Jane interlude.  
  
Social Links were kinda undeveloped. Ruby collected a lot of them but they're sitting in her character sheet like fancy hats. Another goal for ACT 2. We should most likely see Oguri's deal unless you guys choose all the options to avoid her. Maybe she's stinky now. Who knows?  
  
Overall, I'm relatively satisfied with how the plot has turned out so far. The real challenge will be weaving all the individual parts into a cohesive whole. And dealing with my update schedule. I am anal (non-sexual) about editing.  
  
**Mechanics**  
Wow, stumbling blocks. Mostly me afraid of making this impoverished ex-umadol too overpowered. Almost beating Kitasan Black after two months of super intense training still finds like sorta a stretch.  
  
But that's the nature of the AGG and progression fantasy by extension: NUMBERS. POWER. CRUSH THEM ALLLLL. I thought Speed would've been pumped to 12 by now. Persuasion needed more moments to shine. Also, I'm ashamed to admit I totally forgot about Nutrition. That will be remedied soon.  
  
With the latest balance changes, I think we're in a good place. Totally not me tempting fate here.
